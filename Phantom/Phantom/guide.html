PhantomGenesis

The observation that started everything: a rounded rectangle with three visible features and a gap in the corner — and the instinct that all three visible features were phantom, manufactured by the hole, not real. This document develops that instinct into three testable predictions, illustrates them with interactive visualizations (ocean wave drum, circular eigenmode decomposition, rectangular mode grid), derives the exact identities where they are exact, and ends with an honest theorem ledger that distinguishes proved identities from the open global T6 bound. Read this first. It establishes why you should care before explaining how anything works.

Parallel path — toy models

Parallel — not downstream

Intuition and verification in closed form

Not consequences of the main theory — concrete settings where every formula can be inspected in closed form. Accessible at any point from Track A or B. The best place to check that the formulas produce correct numbers before trusting them. Each model adds one structural feature the previous one lacks.

01 — Start here for degeneracy
Quantum Graphs
Star graph: β₁ = (2/3)ρ, β₂ = 0 exactly. Mask-adapted basis has physical meaning — "visits the gapped wire" vs "avoids it." Single-gap measurement is already injective: the forward operator $A = I - B_\lambda$ has eigenvalues $1 - (2/3)\rho$ and $1$, both strictly positive, so $A$ is injective on the antisymmetric eigenspace — even though $\beta_2 = 0$ means one direction of the eigenspace is completely invisible to the gap. A second gap improves conditioning; it does not restore lost injectivity.
02 — Kronecker structure
Rectangular Membrane
K = (4/LW) Jx ⊗ Jy. Every 2D formula reduces to products of closed-form 1D integrals. Structural zeros vs accidental zeros made explicit.
03 — Bessel functions
Circular Disk
Canonical degenerate domain. Closed-form β± for sector gaps. β± computed two independent ways (formula vs polar-grid quadrature, 1.6–2.8% agreement) — a genuine independent cross-check for the degenerate-spectrum identity.
04 — Inner wall effects
Annular Membrane
Disk with inner hole. Bessel + Neumann radial functions. Gap source reflects off inner wall — two-step scattering encoded in Bessel-Neumann combination. Richer radial coupling: inner-wall reflections produce mode interactions absent from solid-disk eigenfunctions.

Known live issues

Currently open or uncertain — as of April 2026

  • T6 full no-cutoff global bound is open [O]. The Gibbs profile Γ(Λt) itself is proved: flat-boundary case T6b is [E] exact, curved-boundary case T6c with microlocal first-reflected cutoff χ₁ is [K]. The remaining open piece is the bound on the bridge term O(Λ⁻¹δ_eff⁻¹) without the χ₁ cutoff — i.e., controlling higher-bounce orbits. T6a outer decay is [K]. The profile model in boundary_layer.ipynb is the proved leading behavior, not a conjecture; only the global-bridge remainder is open.
  • T9 transversality bound is open [O]. The multi-gap aperture synthesis improvement is geometrically correct and numerically verified, but the explicit formula for σmin([A₁;…;AK]) as a function of the principal angle θmin between ambiguity subspaces is still missing.
  • Scattering analogy depth is still being calibrated. What is exact (source on ∂G, coupling is a property of the hole, energy budget exact), what is structural (same eigenbasis pattern), and what is only qualitative analogy (diffraction language, cross-section picture) — the boundaries between these are clearer than in earlier versions but the scattering_analogy.ipynb document still needs one more pass to stop overstating the exactness of the connection.
  • μmax bounds from gap geometry alone: open. Practical sensor placement needs upper bounds on the largest concentration eigenvalue without solving the full eigenvalue problem. No closed-form bound from gap geometry is currently available.
  • No experimental validation yet. All results are analytic or computational. The forward model (gap → phantom spectrum) has not been confirmed against physical measurements on a real system.
